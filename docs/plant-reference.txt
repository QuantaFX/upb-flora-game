1. Pine Tree (Pinus kesiya) - Left side of Inang Laya
Respiratory Care & Expectorant: Fresh needles are heavily utilized in traditional systems across the Himalayas and Southeast Asian hill stations to brew hot teas or steam inhalations. Its volatile monoterpenes possess natural expectorant properties, making it highly effective at relieving deep chest congestion, coughs, and bronchitis. 


2. Tri-vein fern (Christella parasitica) - Front of Inang Laya
Topical Antimicrobial Wash & Dandruff Rinse
The Remedy: Mature fronds featuring distinct orange-colored epidermal glands are boiled gently until the water darkens.
How it is Used: The cooled liquid is used as a medicinal rinse to clean infected wounds, superficial skin lesions, or massaged directly into the scalp as a therapeutic wash.
Medical Action: The specialized glands on the fern's veins secrete active terpenoids and alkaloids. These secondary metabolites naturally break down bacterial membranes, preventing common infectious pathogens (like Staphylococcus aureus) or fungal dandruff spores from replicating on skin tissue. 


3. Gipas (Sarcandra glabra) - Abortion Stairs; between KA and HKP
Infection Control and Wound Care: Cooled decoctions or crushed leaves are applied directly to scalds, burns, and ulcerating wounds. Its broad-spectrum antibacterial traits inhibit common wound-complicating bacteria like Staphylococcus aureus 


4. Kamantigi (Impatiens balsamina) - Abortion Stairs
Topical Antifungal Therapy for Skin Infections
The Remedy: Fresh mature leaves are crushed or pressed to extract their raw, concentrated juices.
How it is Used: The extracted juice is rubbed or dabbed directly onto skin areas affected by superficial fungal infections, such as ringworm (buni) and athlete's foot (alipunga), repeated twice daily.
Medical Action: The plant's leaves contain a powerful bioactive compound called 2-methoxy-1,4-naphthoquinone (MNQ). Modern pharmacological screenings confirm that MNQ exerts robust antifungal activity that breaks down fungal cell walls, effectively clearing stubborn dermatomycoses.


   5. Fishtail fern (Microsorum punctatum) - between KA and HKP
Urination Support & Diuretic Action: In several South and Southeast Asian traditional systems (such as in Assam, India), raw juices or liquid infusions extracted from the fresh fronds are taken internally to manage urinary tract disorders and difficulties. The extract acts as a natural diuretic, increasing urine output to help flush irritants from the bladder and kidneys. 


   6. Moclame (Ficus microcapra) - between KA and HKP
Pain Relief & Fever Management: In East Asian folk traditions (such as in Japan and China), decoctions made from the bark, dried leaves, and roots are administered to alleviate fevers, severe headaches, and bodily pain caused by influenza or malaria. 


   7. Agoho (Casuarina equisetifolia) - near CS AVR
Topical Acne and Pimple Therapy: In regional Southeast Asian traditions, the bark is ground down into a fine, dry powder. This powder is mixed with a small amount of clean water to form a localized spot paste used to clear up severe acne and facial pimples. Its antimicrobial compounds suppress common skin bacteria like Staphylococcus aureus.
